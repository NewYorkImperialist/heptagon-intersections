# (R3) — the residue of §9.1's "One final point" step

Target: condition **(R3)** of [`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md) Finding 4 — *"no
removable degeneracy left"* — the one of the three reduction hypotheses that
document leaves open, and whose Zariski-genericity sketch it explicitly records
as **"reasoning, not a finding"**, flagging two unchecked objections:

1. AKR's **Theorem 17 determines** the dependent vertices' directions from the
   free ones, so the direction tuple lives in a linear subspace, not in
   `(ℝ²)¹⁴`; a genericity argument must be run *inside* that subspace.
2. AKR **force `E_i = 0`** for free vertices not involved in a degeneracy
   ("Free vertices that are not involved in a degeneracy stay where they are"),
   a further and very restrictive constraint.

All arithmetic below is exact `fractions.Fraction`, and the direction tuple is
carried **symbolically** (exact multivariate polynomials over ℚ in the free
direction coordinates), so statements of the form "vanishes for *every*
admissible direction" are decided by polynomial identity, not by sampling.
Nothing is floating point. No Lean build was run.

**This does not prove `f(7,7) = 38`.** That remains a candidate resting on the
unreviewed §9 import.

Code: [`r3lib.py`](r3lib.py) (multivariate polynomials, Theorem 17 solver),
[`r3.py`](r3.py) (configurations, witness family, sweeps),

## Finding R3-1 — an AKR-admissible direction can make `∂P(ε) ∩ ∂Q(ε)` **infinite**

This is the first result, and it is a strictly worse failure than `FINDINGS.md`
Finding 2. Finding 2 exhibits an AKR-admissible direction that leaves a
vertex-**side** degeneracy behind, and argues that this is survivable because
§9.2 exists to remove vertex-side degeneracies. The certificate below destroys
§9.2's *hypothesis*: AKR's standing finiteness assumption (F), which §9 inherits
from §1, holds at `ε = 0` and **fails for every `ε > 0`**. §9.2 cannot repair
that — [`../../docs/akr-9-2-obligation.md`](../../docs/akr-9-2-obligation.md)
§2.3 lists (F) as a hypothesis of the averaging claim, and §5.8 records that
finiteness is "assumed, never discharged, in §9.2 as in §1 and §3".

### The mechanism

At a coincidence `P_i = Q_j` let `a₁ = P_{i−1} − P_i` and `b₁ = Q_{j−1} − Q_j`
be **antiparallel**. Then the P-side `[P_i,P_{i−1}]` and the Q-side
`[Q_j,Q_{j−1}]` are collinear and meet in exactly the apex — so (F) holds at
`ε = 0`, and `e9_directions`' criterion (*not all four rays mutually parallel*)
is satisfied, since `a₂` and `b₂` are free. Now move `P_i` **along `b₁`**. The
two collinear sides slide into an overlap of length `∝ ε`, and
`∂P(ε) ∩ ∂Q(ε)` becomes infinite.

Take `E = (2,0)`.

* **`E` is inside an `e9_directions` cone**, checked exactly: with
  `a_k = a₂ = (0,−3)` and `b_l = b₂ = (2,−1)`, `E = t·b_l − s·a_k` with
  `t = 1 > 0` and `s = 1/3 > 0`. So it is not a direction the `e9_directions`
  construction would reject — it is one that construction *produces*.
* **AKR's own criterion (R2) is met**: the side pair
  `([P₀(ε),P₁], [Q₀,Q₁])` has all four `orient` polynomials `≢ 0` and their

## Finding R3-2 — the two objections do **not** break genericity, and here is the proof

This is the part `FINDINGS.md` records as "reasoning, not a finding". It is now
a proof, run inside the subspace, with the `E = 0` rule in force.

### Setup and notation

`V⁰ ∈ (ℝ²)¹⁴` are the combined vertices of simple polygons `P⁰, Q⁰` with
`∂P⁰ ∩ ∂Q⁰` finite (AKR's standing hypotheses). Let

* `C` = the set of vertex-vertex coincidences `(P_i, Q_j)`;
* `D` = the **dependent** vertices, i.e. those lying in the relative interior of
  a side of the *other* polygon, with relation (1) `A = λB + (1−λ)C`;
* `F = [14] ∖ D` the free vertices;
* `W` the 1638-element witness family of `FINDINGS.md` Finding 3.

AKR's rule, written out: `E_{P_i}` is chosen for each `(P_i,Q_j) ∈ C`;
`E_{Q_j} = 0`; `E_v = 0` for every other free `v`; and `E_d` for `d ∈ D` is
**determined** by Theorem 17's system (2)–(3).

### Lemma R3-A — a coincident vertex is always free

> If `P⁰, Q⁰` are simple and `P_i = Q_j`, then `P_i ∉ D` and `Q_j ∉ D`.

*Proof.* `P_i ∈ D` means `P_i` lies in the relative interior of a side of `Q⁰`.
But `P_i = Q_j` is a **vertex** of `Q⁰`. A vertex of a simple polygon is an
endpoint, not an interior point, of its two incident sides, and lies on no other
side at all (that would be a self-touching of `∂Q⁰`). Symmetrically for `Q_j`. ∎

AKR never state this, yet their sentence "we leave `Q_j` stationary but we move
`P_i = P⁰_i + εE_i`" **presupposes** it: it assigns `P_i` a freely chosen
direction, which is only meaningful for a free vertex. Lemma R3-A is what makes
the sentence well posed, and it is the hinge of everything below.

*Checked:* in the 1 220 generated configurations of the sweeps in §R3-4, the
detector found a coincident vertex that was also dependent **0 times**, and
`sanity()` would have rejected the configuration if it had.

### Lemma R3-B — the direction space is a linear space, coordinatised by `C`

> The assignment `(E_{P_i})_{(i,j)∈C} ↦ (E_v)_{v=1..14}` is a well-defined,
> injective **linear** map `Λ : (ℝ²)^C → (ℝ²)¹⁴`. Write `L := im Λ`,
> `dim L = 2·|C|`.

*Proof.* Theorem 17's system (2)–(3) is, in the `x`-coordinates and in the
`y`-coordinates, the *same* `|D|×|D|` matrix (its entries are the `λ`'s) with
different right-hand sides; Theorem 17 says it is nonsingular. So
`E_d = Σ_{f ∈ F} α_{d,f} E_f` with **scalar** rational `α` depending only on the
`λ`'s. Substituting AKR's `E_f = 0` for `f ∈ F ∖ C` (legitimate by Lemma R3-A,
which puts every `P_i` of a coincidence in `F`) gives
`E_d = Σ_{(i,j)∈C} α_{d,i} E_{P_i}`. Everything is linear, and `Λ` restricted to
the `C`-coordinates is the identity, so `Λ` is injective. ∎

`L` is **exactly** the subspace the two objections describe: objection 1 is the
statement that the `d ∈ D` coordinates are determined, objection 2 that the
`F ∖ C` coordinates are zero. `r3lib.theorem17_directions` solves this system
exactly and reports singularity rather than assuming Theorem 17.

### Lemma R3-C — the admissible set is open and nonempty **in `L`**

Let `C_{ij} ⊆ ℝ² ∖ {0}` be an `e9_directions` cone at `(P_i,Q_j)`:
`C_{ij} = {t·b_l − s·a_k : s,t > 0}` for a pair with `a_k ∦ b_l`, nonempty
because AKR's finiteness assumption forbids all four rays being parallel
(`e9_directions` Corollary). Put `A := Λ(∏_{(i,j)∈C} C_{ij})`.

> `A` is a nonempty **open** subset of `L`, it has positive Lebesgue measure in
> `L`, and every `E ∈ A` satisfies (R1) and (R2).

*Proof.* `∏ C_{ij}` is a nonempty open subset of `(ℝ²)^C` — the cones live in
*different* coordinates, because `P_i` determines `Q_j` (`Q⁰` simple) and `Q_j`
determines `P_i`, so no `P`-vertex carries two cone constraints. `Λ` is a linear
isomorphism onto `L`, hence a homeomorphism, so `A` is open in `L` and nonempty.
(R1): `0 ∉ C_{ij}` since `t b_l − s a_k = 0` with `a_k ∦ b_l` forces `s = t = 0`;
so `E_{P_i} ≠ 0 = E_{Q_j}`. (R2): `e9_directions`' cone lemma, upgraded to the
simultaneous setting by `FINDINGS.md` Finding 1 (the leading coefficients at an
apex do not involve the neighbours' directions). ∎

### Theorem R3 — genericity survives both objections

Write `Z(E) := { p ∈ W : p(·;E) ≡ 0 in ε }` and
`Z_forced := ⋂_{E ∈ L} Z(E)`.

> **(i)** `⋂_{E ∈ A} Z(E) = Z_forced`.
> **(ii)** `{ E ∈ A : Z(E) = Z_forced }` is a **full-measure** subset of `A`,
> in particular nonempty. Explicitly: there is an admissible direction tuple —
> satisfying Theorem 17's determination, AKR's `E = 0` rule and AKR's own
> criterion (R2) simultaneously — for which **every** identically-zero witness
> is forced, i.e. (R3) holds.

*Proof.* Coordinatise `L` by `Λ`, so `L ≅ ℝ^{2|C|}`. Fix `p ∈ W` and write
`p = c₀ + c₁(E)ε + c₂(E)ε²`. By Lemma R3-B each `E_v` is a **linear** function
of the coordinates of `L`, and `orient`/`⟨·,·⟩`/difference are bilinear or
linear in the vertex positions; so `c₀` is a constant, `c₁` a linear form on `L`
and `c₂` a quadratic form on `L`. Hence

  `p ∈ Z(E) ⟺ c₀ = 0 and c₁(E) = 0 and c₂(E) = 0`,

so `p ∈ Z_forced ⟺ c₀ = 0` and `c₁, c₂` are the **zero polynomials** on `L`.

For `p ∉ Z_forced` put `B_p := { E ∈ L : c₁(E) = c₂(E) = 0 }` if `c₀ = 0`, and
`B_p := ∅` if `c₀ ≠ 0`. In the first case at least one of `c₁, c₂` is a nonzero
polynomial on `L`, so `B_p` is contained in the zero set of a nonzero polynomial
on `L ≅ ℝ^{2|C|}`: closed, Lebesgue-null, with empty interior.
`B := ⋃_{p ∈ W ∖ Z_forced} B_p` is a union of at most 1638 such sets, hence
Lebesgue-null and with empty interior in `L`.

(i) If `p ∉ Z_forced` then `B_p` has empty interior in `L`, while `A` is
nonempty open in `L` (Lemma R3-C), so `A ⊄ B_p`, i.e. some admissible `E` has
`p ∉ Z(E)`. Hence `⋂_{E∈A} Z(E) ⊆ Z_forced`; the reverse inclusion is trivial.

(ii) `A` is nonempty open in `L`, hence of positive Lebesgue measure; `B` is
null; so `A ∖ B` has full measure in `A` and is in particular nonempty, and
every `E ∈ A ∖ B` has `Z(E) = Z_forced`. ∎

### Why the two objections cannot bite

The proof uses **nothing** about the ambient space except that it is a *linear*
(hence irreducible) space in which `A` is open and nonempty. Objection 1
(Theorem 17 determines `E_d`) and objection 2 (`E_v = 0` off `C`) are both
**linear** constraints, so they replace `(ℝ²)¹⁴` by the smaller linear space `L`
and change nothing else. The previous agent's worry — "the bad set might contain
the subspace" — is exactly the possibility that some `p` lies in `Z_forced`; it
is not a failure of genericity but a *redefinition of the target*. Shrinking `L`
can only **enlarge** `Z_forced`, never make the target unreachable.

So the whole content migrates to one question: **is `Z_forced` small enough?**
That is Finding R3-3.

## Finding R3-3 — `Z_forced` is harmless: no *required* witness is ever forced

Theorem R3 says (R3) is achievable. It does not say the achievable target is
good enough. This section pins down what `Z_forced` can contain, and proves
that the witnesses §9.1's own deliverables need are never in it. **The proofs
are unconditional — they hold for every configuration satisfying AKR's standing
hypotheses, not for a sample.**

### What must be non-identically-zero, and what may legitimately stay zero

| witness | status | why |
|---|---|---|
| `Δˣ_{ij}, Δʸ_{ij}` for `(P_i,Q_j) ∈ C` | **required** (not both zero) | (G1): the coincidence must separate |
| the four `D`'s of a side pair realising (R2) at each coincidence | **required** | (G2c): a crossing must appear in place of the coincidence |
| the four `D`'s of a `P`-side / `Q`-side pair **collinear at `ε=0` and meeting** | **required** (not all zero) | otherwise the two sides slide into a positive-length overlap: Finding R3-1 |
| `D_{ijk}` for a relation-(1) triple `(A,B,C)` | **may stay zero** | Theorem 17 maintains it on purpose; it is §9.2's job (`docs/akr-9-2-obligation.md` §2.3) |
| `D_{ijk}` for three vertices AKR's rule keeps stationary, collinear at `ε=0` | **may stay zero** | nothing can move them. Includes a straight vertex of one polygon — `lean/AKR9.lean`'s `SimpleClosed` **deliberately allows** these (`akr-9-2-obligation.md` §6.1), so §9.1 need not remove them. They do break §9.2's unstated hypothesis **(NC)**, which is that document's Gap 1, already recorded with a repair |
| `B_{i;jk}` for three stationary vertices orthogonal at `ε=0` | **may stay zero** | `B` only decides betweenness *along a collinear triple*; an accidental right angle among frozen points is not a degeneracy |
| `Δˣ` (or `Δʸ`) alone, for two stationary vertices sharing one coordinate | **may stay zero** | a shared `x`-coordinate is not a coincidence |
| a `D`, `B` or `Δ` of a side pair **collinear at `ε=0` but disjoint** | **may stay zero** | disjointness of two compact sets is an *open* condition; bootstrapping (`FINDINGS.md` Finding 4) gives it on all of `(0,ε₀)` without any first-order input |

### Lemma R3-E — a collinear side pair can only meet at a coincidence

> Let `s` be a `P⁰`-side and `t` a `Q⁰`-side with all four endpoints on one line
> `ℓ`, and suppose `s ∩ t ≠ ∅`. Under (F), `s ∩ t` is a single point which is an
> endpoint of both — i.e. a vertex-vertex coincidence.

*Proof.* Two collinear segments meet in a segment or a point. A positive-length
segment makes `∂P⁰ ∩ ∂Q⁰` infinite, contradicting (F). So `s ∩ t = {x}`. If `x`
were in the relative interior of `s`, then `s ⊇ [x−ηd, x+ηd]` for some `η>0` and
a unit `d` along `ℓ`; and `t`, being a nondegenerate segment of `ℓ` containing
`x`, contains `[x, x+δd]` or `[x−δd, x]` for some `δ>0`. Either way `s ∩ t`
contains a segment of length `min(η,δ) > 0`. Contradiction. So `x` is an
endpoint of `s`, and symmetrically of `t`. ∎

### Theorem R3-D — the two identities that make the required witnesses safe

> Let `(P_i,Q_j) ∈ C`, put `V = V⁰_{P_i} = V⁰_{Q_j}`, `e := E_{P_i}` (a free
> coordinate pair of `L`, by Lemma R3-A), and `u⁰_k := V⁰_{P_{i+k}} − V` for
> `k = ±1`. Under AKR's rule (`E_{Q_j} = 0`):
>
> **(a)** `Δ_{ij}(ε) = ε·e`.
> **(b)** `orient(P_i(ε), P_{i+k}(ε), Q_j(ε))
>          = −ε·cross(u⁰_k, e) − ε²·cross(E_{P_{i+k}} − e, e)`.
>
> In particular the `ε¹`-coefficients `e` and `−cross(u⁰_k, e)` are **nonzero**
> polynomials on `L`, because `u⁰_k ≠ 0` and `e` is an unconstrained coordinate
> pair of `L`. Hence none of `Δˣ_{ij}`, `Δʸ_{ij}`, `D(P_i,P_{i+k},Q_j)` lies in
> `Z_forced`, for **any** configuration.

*Proof.* (a) `Δ_{ij}(ε) = (V + εe) − (V + 0) = εe`.
(b) `orient(A,B,C) = cross(B−A, C−A)` with `A = V+εe`, `B = V+u⁰_k+εE_{P_{i+k}}`,
`C = V`. So `B−A = u⁰_k + ε(E_{P_{i+k}}−e)` and `C−A = −εe`, and
`cross(u⁰_k + ε(E_{P_{i+k}}−e), −εe) = −ε·cross(u⁰_k,e) − ε²·cross(E_{P_{i+k}}−e,e)`. ∎

### Corollary R3-D′ — (R1) and the finiteness requirement are never forced-broken

*(R1)* is (a): `Δˣ_{ij}` and `Δʸ_{ij}` are `ε·x_e` and `ε·y_e`, coordinate forms
on `L`; and on `A`, `e ≠ 0` (Lemma R3-C), so at least one is nonzero at `ε>0`.

*Finiteness.* Let a `P`-side `s` and a `Q`-side `t` be collinear at `ε=0` and
meet. By Lemma R3-E they meet at a coincidence `P_i = Q_j`, and `P_{i+k}` is the
other endpoint of `s` for one of `k = ±1`. By (b), `D(P_i,P_{i+k},Q_j) ∉
Z_forced`. Since that triple is one of the four the requirement lists, the
requirement is satisfiable, and Theorem R3 realises it simultaneously with all
the others. ∎

### Theorem R3-F — §9.1's output is finite, for every `ε ∈ (0,ε₀)`

> Add to `FINDINGS.md`'s deliverables (G1)–(G4) the clause
> **(G5)** `∂P(ε) ∩ ∂Q(ε)` is finite.
> Under (Aff), (R1), (R2), (R3) and AKR's standing hypotheses, (G5) holds for
> every `ε ∈ (0, ε₀)`.

*Proof.* `∂P(ε) ∩ ∂Q(ε)` is infinite iff some `P`-side and `Q`-side overlap in a
positive-length segment, which forces them to be collinear. Take any pair
`(s,t)`.
* If they are **not collinear at `ε=0`**, some `D` of the four endpoints has
  `c₀ ≠ 0`, so by Theorem U it is nonzero on `(0,ε₀)` and they are never
  collinear there: they meet in at most one point.
* If they are **collinear at `ε=0` and disjoint**, they are two disjoint compact
  sets, hence at positive distance; disjointness is a Boolean function of the
  sign vector of `W`; so by bootstrapping they are disjoint on all of `(0,ε₀)`.
* If they are **collinear at `ε=0` and meet**, Corollary R3-D′ plus (R3) give a
  `D` among the four that is `≢ 0`, hence (Theorem U) nonzero on `(0,ε₀)`: they
  are not collinear there, so they meet in at most one point. ∎

**(G5) is new.** `FINDINGS.md`'s Reduction Theorem does not list it, and §9.2
*assumes* it (`akr-9-2-obligation.md` §2.3 hypothesis **(F)**, §5.8 "assumed,
never discharged"). Finding R3-1 shows it is not automatic: it is the precise
place where (R3) is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

### 1. Lemma R3-C's nonemptiness clause cites a refuted proof — repair

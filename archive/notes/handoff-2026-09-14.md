### 8.1 The judgement call, and why

Obligation (4) is blocked on eight named hypotheses (E1)–(E8). Six of them are
*external mathematics* — the published Černý and AKR lemmas, and the exceptional
classification — and closing those means formalizing other people's papers. But
**two are not mathematics at all**:

* **(E6)** correctness of the union-find **component labelling**;
* **(E8)** correctness of the **bipartiteness routine**.

Both are standard algorithm-correctness facts over a 14-vertex graph, provable
outright in Lean with no external input. Discharging them takes obligation (4)
from eight open hypotheses to six, and — more importantly — removes the two that
a reviewer would rightly find *embarrassing* to be assuming, since they are
properties of this project's own code rather than of the literature. That is
where the next effort went.

### 8.2 `UB42` verified independently, and queued for Lean

While that ran I checked the `I = 42` exclusion, which is the other branch of the
upper bound and is **project-original** (not an external lemma). Its argument:
`I = 42` forces `|E| = 7`; with minimum degree ≥ 1 on 7+7 vertices that makes
`G^D` a **perfect matching**; Lemma A with `|S| = 4` forces both heptagons to be
exceptional; each `q_j` then crosses exactly `{0..6} \ {i_j}`, a 6-set; every
exceptional class's maximal crossing sets are exactly `{FULL \ {m} : m ∈ M}` with
`|M| = 5`; two 6-subsets of a 7-set nest only if equal, so every `i_j ∈ M`; the
`i_j` are distinct because `G^D` is a matching, so all seven sides lie in `M`,
contradicting `|M| = 5`.

I re-verified both computational ingredients from the class data directly, rather
than reading the stored invariant block:

* the invariant `|M| = 5` **and** `maximal_crossing_sets = {FULL \ {m} : m ∈ M}`
  holds for **all 14** classes;
* the exhaustive sweep over all `7! = 5040` perfect matchings against all 14
  classes leaves **0 of 70,560** survivors.

This matters because **most of `UB42` is pure combinatorics** — the perfect-matching
step, the nesting step, and the counting contradiction are all Lean-provable now,
with Lemma A already formalized (`lean/LemmaA.lean`) and only the class invariant
needing to enter as data or hypothesis. It is the most tractable remaining piece
of the upper bound and is queued next.

### 8.3 The upper-bound skeleton is now a theorem (`lean/UpperBoundAssembly.lean`)

The upper-bound argument is a short chain resting on a few large results, but
the chain itself lived only in prose, so a reader could not tell which links were
machine-checked and which were carrying the weight. It is now a Lean theorem.
Axioms: `propext`, `Quot.sound` — **no `Classical.choice`**.

```
(1) I + |E| = 49                     PROVED for every Config
(2) minimum degree >= 1 on P side    PROVED for every Config
(3) hence |E| >= 7, hence I <= 42    proved here, UNCONDITIONALLY
(4) I is even                        HYPOTHESIS
(5) I != 42                          HYPOTHESIS  (UB42)
(6) I != 40                          HYPOTHESIS  (UB40 — the enumeration)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
    I <= 38
```

**`crossCountC_le_42` is unconditional** — for *every* pair of simple heptagons
in general position, `I ≤ 42`, kernel-checked. This is the project's use of
Černý L1, and note it is not imported: the minimum-degree fact it rests on was
*derived* here from the parity argument in `SegmentGeometry.lean`. As far as I
can tell this is the first unconditional upper bound on `I` proved in Lean for
arbitrary configurations rather than for the certificate pair.

Step (3) is also what makes 42 and 40 the *only* two even values needing
exclusion, which is why the whole upper bound reduces to exactly two branches.

**What this does and does not say.** It does not prove the upper bound. It
proves that those three facts *suffice*, and that (1)–(3) are already done. The
value is that the two open branches are now visible as two hypotheses in one
statement instead of being distributed through prose.

One honest correction worth recording: I initially expected parity — "I is even"
— to be available from the geometry group. It is not.
`SegmentGeometry.metCount_even` is a *different* parity fact (a closed polygon
meets a **line** an even number of times), which is what feeds step (2). It does
not give step (4), so `heven` is a hypothesis.

Non-vacuity is proved, not asserted: the certificate pair satisfies all three
hypotheses, and `cert_le_38` is obtained by *applying the general theorem*
rather than by `decide` on the conclusion — with `cert_attains_38` showing the

### 8.10 Can `hne40` be closed in the kernel at all? — measured, then corrected

With parity proved, everything turns on `hne40`, which requires certifying a
search over 13,003,620 graphs. `native_decide` is excluded by the stated goal (it
adds `Lean.ofReduceBool`). So: can the kernel do it?

**Measured.** Kernel `decide` on trivial per-element work runs at about **2,000
elements/second** (10,000 in ~6.2 s, 20,000 in ~12.6 s, clean linear scaling). The
project's own `enumRec` at depth 3 — 5,670 leaves, ~1/2,300 of the full run — did
**not finish in ten minutes** of kernel reduction.

**Then corrected, twice, both times against myself.** I first wrote in
`roadmap.md` §8 that this settles it. It does not:

1. `enumRec` returns `List (List Nat)`, so that depth-3 figure is dominated by
   *building list terms*, not by arithmetic. A **counting** formulation that
   accumulates a `Nat` is a different computation and was never benchmarked.
2. My attempt to benchmark one was **invalid**: I iterated all 128 masks at each
   of seven levels, dropping the real algorithm's popcount-indexed `SUBS`
   iteration and its `min(3, budget−(rem−1))` prune. That explores a far bigger
   tree — Python could not finish it either. It measured a worse algorithm and is
   evidence about nothing.

Calibration the other way: the real Python `rec`, *with the full filter
pipeline*, sweeps all 13,003,620 graphs in about **190 seconds**. At the ~500×
kernel-vs-Python ratio the trivial-work measurement suggests, a well-engineered
counting formulation lands near **a day** — absurd as a build step, not obviously
impossible as a one-off.

**Honest position: the barrier is real; its magnitude is not established.** My
own estimates span orders of magnitude. A rigorous benchmark of a properly
engineered counting formulation (popcount-indexed iteration, the real prune,
`Nat` accumulator, no list construction) is running now, and should be believed
over anything above.

### 8.11 A strategy that was tested and does *not* work

`UB42` was tractable because an exhaustive 70,560-case sweep was replaced by a
structural argument using only Lemma A and the *cheap* class invariants
(`|M| = 5`; maximal crossing sets of the form `FULL \ {m}`). The obvious hope was
that the same trick would cut `I = 40`.

**It does not.** Applying exactly those cheap invariants to the `I = 40` domain
cuts 13,003,620 → **8,717,100** — a 33% reduction — and barely dents the
degree-3 branch (4,086,600 → 3,028,200). Measured, not estimated.

So the external lemmas are genuinely load-bearing for `I = 40`, and the
`UB42`-style restructuring hope should be considered tested and closed rather
than left open as a plan. This is consistent with the K-17 correction recorded in `known-issues.md`:
the degree-3 branch survives every graph-level filter and dies only at Černý L2.

### 8.13 The feasibility question is answered: memory, not time

> **SUPERSEDED BY §10.1 — note added 2026-09-14.** The verdict below is the
> *monolithic* measurement. §8b then showed chunking bounds peak memory at ~2 GB,
> removing the memory wall; and §8c measured the number that actually decides the

The benchmark §8.10 called for has been run rigorously. Full report and all
thirteen scratch Lean files preserved at
[`results/benchmarks/2026-09-14_kernel-enumeration-feasibility.md`](../results/benchmarks/2026-09-14_kernel-enumeration-feasibility.md).

The full traversal is **226,668,772 recursive calls**. Measured, `Nat`, completed
runs only:

| calls | user s | wall s | peak RSS |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 88,905 | 20.86 | 21.12 | 2.42 GB |
| 134,030 | 32.65 | **173.80** | 2.68 GB |
| 228,061 | — | **did not finish in 180 s** | — |

* **CPU time is linear** and projects to **≈15 CPU-hours** (band 13–16).
* **Wall time is not.** At 134,030 calls wall is 5.3× CPU — already swapping —
  and the linear model has broken before reaching **0.06%** of the full tree.
* **Memory binds.** ~20–25 KB peak RSS per call extrapolates to **4.5–5.8 TB**,
  some **560–720× the 8 GB installed**. Illustrative, not measured — but `vm_stat`
  showed ~19.75 GB in the system compressor during a large run.

Two useful negative results: `UInt8` masks were **1.8–2.0× slower** than `Nat`
and used more memory, so the obvious optimisation backfires; `foldr` did not help.

**Verdict: not feasible here, and the obstacle is memory rather than time.**
Fifteen CPU-hours would be an acceptable one-off; 4.5 TB resident is not. Three
things stop this being over-read: it is a resource cliff for **one formulation**,
not a proof that every kernel route fails; the 15 hours is for **counting only**
and evaluates no filter, while `hne40` needs L5, L7, compatibility and Černý L2
applied per graph; and a much larger machine might simply finish it.

**And the restructuring escape routes are closing.** Three have now been tested:

| route | result |
|---|---|
| `UB42`-style argument from cheap class invariants | **dead** — cuts only 33% (13,003,620 → 8,717,100) |
| lead with the *local* filters (compatibility first) | **dead** — leaves 2,783,508, against the 42,238 AKR L5 alone achieves |
| carry a candidate component partition in a row DP | untested — but enlarges state, and **memory** is what binds |

### 8.16 I3 closed — the `avoiding` flag is no longer trusted

`lean/AvoidingFlag.lean`. The reviewer guide called this "the smallest real gap
in the Lean development", and it is now closed.

`Endgame.lean` used a stored Boolean `Case.avoiding`, copied from a JSON field,
which Lean simply trusted — a record carrying a wrong flag would have passed
every theorem in the file. Now:

```lean
all_225_avoiding_flags_eq :
  ∀ c ∈ certificates, c.avoiding = decide ((c.pairI, c.pairJ) ∈ avoidingPairs c.classUsed)
```

Note it is an **equality**, so the `false` case is checked too — not the weaker
implication that would leave a spuriously-`false` flag unexamined. Axioms
`propext`, `Quot.sound`.

The class table is **generated from** `exceptional_profiles_14.json` rather than
hand-typed, and I checked independently that all 14 classes match the JSON
exactly before accepting it — a wrong table would have made the theorem
meaningless rather than false.

**What is closed and what is not.** The flag/table consistency gap is gone: the
stored flag is no longer an independent failure mode. The *geometric* half of I3
remains wide open — correctness and completeness of the exceptional
classification (E7), the correspondence between a record's class indices and an
actual polygon, and a geometric definition of "avoiding", which this development
does not have at all.

## 10. Final position: `hne40` is settled negative, and what that means

### 10.1 The engineering question is closed

Five routes to closing `hne40` inside the kernel have been tested. All are dead,
and each has a measured number rather than an argument:

| route | verdict | number |
|---|---|---|
| monolithic evaluation | dead — memory | ~4.5 TB extrapolated |

worth having, because it stops anyone spending months rediscovering it.

### 10.2 What is *not* settled

A **mathematical** restructuring that shrinks the 13,003,620-element domain.
Three attempts failed, but three failures do not exhaust the space of arguments.
`UB42` is the existence proof that this can work: an exhaustive 70,560-case sweep
was replaced by a perfect-matching step, a one-line nesting argument and a
counting contradiction that the kernel checks in milliseconds. Something similar
may exist for `I = 40`. That is mathematics, not engineering, and it is precisely
where a human expert is worth more than another benchmark.

### 10.3 What "rock solid" now means for this project

The result is a **machine-checked reduction**, and its solidity rests on four

\begin{abstract}
This document is a dependency manifest, not a paper. It states exactly what the
candidate proof of $f(7,7)=38$ consists of, and separates published external
mathematics from project-original arguments, computational reductions, external
database checks, certificate verification, Lean-kernel verification, and the
items still requiring human review. Every claim carries a source and a
verification status. It is written so that a referee can locate the weakest link
quickly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim and current status}

Let $f(m,n)$ denote the maximum number of boundary intersection points of two
simple polygons with $m$ and $n$ sides in general position.

\begin{theorem}[Claimed]\label{thm:main}
$f(7,7)=38$.
\end{theorem}

The problem is due to \cite{DillencourtMountSaalfeld1993}, who asked for
$f(m,n)$ in general and settled the even cases. The odd--odd cases have resisted;
a 2012 preprint claiming $f(n,n)$ for odd $n$ \cite{Gunther2012} was withdrawn by
its author as faulty and is not relied on here.

Three facts frame the problem.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{$38$ is realizable.} An explicit pair of integer-coordinate
    heptagons attains $38$ crossings (\S\ref{sec:lean}, \textsf{LB38}); this is
    verified by the Lean kernel.
  \item \textbf{The published state of the art leaves only $40$.}
    {\v C}ern{\'y} et al.~\cite[Theorem 4]{CernyEtAl2003} prove
    $f(k,\ell)\le k\ell-\lceil k/6\rceil-\ell$ for odd $7\le k\le\ell$, which at
    $k=\ell=7$ gives $f(7,7)\le 40$; \cite{AckermanKeszeghRote2022} is
    asymptotically optimal but vacuous at $m=n=7$. Crossings of two closed curves
    in general position are even, so $f(7,7)\in\{38,40\}$ and the whole question
    is whether $40$ is attainable.

    \emph{This bound is context, not a dependency.} Importing it would shorten
    the argument --- $I\le40$, $I\neq40$ and parity would give $I\le38$ at once,
    with no $I=42$ case --- but \cite[Theorem 4]{CernyEtAl2003} rests on
    \cite[Lemma 3]{CernyEtAl2003}, a four-page case analysis this project has not
    audited, and importing it would contradict the project's own accounting of
    its dependencies. The argument here instead uses only
    \cite[Lemma 1]{CernyEtAl2003} (\textsf{C1}), which gives the weaker cap
    $I\le42$, and excludes $I=42$ separately in \S\ref{sec:i42}. {\v C}ern{\'y}
    Theorem 4 is therefore independent corroboration of the upper bound, not an
    input to it.
  \item \textbf{The upper bound is a proof by contradiction.} One assumes a pair
    with $I=40$ and derives a contradiction. Everything in
    \S\ref{sec:flow}--\S\ref{sec:cert} serves that single reductio.
\end{enumerate}

We do \emph{not} claim Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is established. We claim that no
computational gap is currently known, and that the remaining risk is
concentrated in one identified place (\textsf{A7-EXT}, \S\ref{sec:a7ext}).

\subsection{What is a Lean theorem today, and what is not}\label{sec:leanmain}

The Lean development (\S\ref{sec:lean}) is large enough that a reader can
mistake its coverage for coverage of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. It is not. The
strongest statement it contains is

{\footnotesize\begin{verbatim}
theorem IntersectionParity.isGreatest_38_final
    (hclass : forall K : Config, exists mcs MP, MP.card = 5 /\ ... ) --  X1 + X2
    (hne40  : forall K : Config, crossCountC K <> 40)                --  UB40
    : IsGreatest MainTheorem.attained 38
\end{verbatim}}

\noindent
and it still carries those \emph{two hypotheses}. Neither is proved anywhere in
the development:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{hclass} is the exceptional-class invariant together with the
    order-type crossing fact --- \textsf{X1} and \textsf{X2} of
    \S\ref{sec:lean}, i.e.\ \textsf{EX} and \textsf{E4} --- asserted for
    \emph{every} configuration;
  \item \texttt{hne40} is $I\neq40$, that is the entire $13{,}003{,}620$-graph
    enumeration, \textsf{UB40}. It is \textbf{not} reachable by the Lean kernel
    on this architecture; five routes were tried and all are dead
    (\S\ref{sec:hne40}).
\end{itemize}

Two further gaps sit outside the theorem statement and are not visible in it.
\texttt{Config} is an \emph{orientation-level} object over
$\texttt{Pt}:=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$: ``simple'' means a conjunction of
integer orientation predicates, not ``the closed polygonal curve is a Jordan
curve''. And the reduction from arbitrary position to general position is
\textsf{GP}, \textsc{akr} \S9.1, which is cited and not formalised. So even with
both hypotheses discharged, the theorem would read ``$38$ is the maximum
crossing count over integer-coordinate, orientation-level heptagon
configurations'', and two further external steps would be needed to reach
$f(7,7)=38$.

What \emph{is} unconditional, for an arbitrary \texttt{Config}, is the
following, and it is worth stating because it is a genuine bracket:
\[
  I+|\ED| = 49, \qquad \delta(\GD)\ge1, \qquad I\le42, \qquad I \text{ even},
  \qquad 38 \in \text{attained},
\]
so the answer lies in $\{38,40,42\}$ with no hypotheses at all
(\texttt{MainTheorem.attained\_38\_and\_le\_42},
\texttt{attained\_mem\_three}). Adding \texttt{hclass} removes $42$
(\texttt{Exclude42.not\_crossCount\_42}). Only $40$ resists.

\section{One-page proof flow}\label{sec:flow}

Write $\GD$ for the bipartite \emph{disjointness graph} on the $7+7$ sides, with
$(p_i,q_j)\in\ED$ iff the closed segments $p_i$ and $q_j$ are disjoint. Since two
non-collinear segments meet at most once, crossings biject with intersecting side
pairs, so
\[
  I + |\ED| = 49 .
\]

The reductio proceeds as follows; the label under each arrow names the
justification.
{\small\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
  I=40
  &\stackrel{\text{\footnotesize $I+|\ED|=49$}}{\Longrightarrow}&
  |\ED| = 9 \\[4pt]
  &\stackrel{\text{\footnotesize \textsf{C1} ({\v C}ern{\'y} L1)}}{\Longrightarrow}&
  \delta(\GD)\ge 1 \\[4pt]
  &\stackrel{\text{\footnotesize \textsf{LA} (Lemma A + parity)}}{\Longrightarrow}&
  P \text{ and } Q \text{ are both \emph{exceptional}} \\[4pt]
  &\stackrel{\text{\footnotesize \textsf{OT},\textsf{EX} (order-type classification)}}{\Longrightarrow}&
  (\text{profile}_P,\text{profile}_Q) \in \{14\}\times\{14\} \\[4pt]
  &\stackrel{\text{\footnotesize \textsf{GR},\textsf{COMP} (enumeration; \textsf{A5-C},\textsf{A7-EXT},\textsf{C2})}}{\Longrightarrow}&
  225 \text{ terminal triples on } 121 \text{ graphs} \\[4pt]
  &\stackrel{\text{\footnotesize \textsf{A5-A},\textsf{CERT},\textsf{LEAN-END}}}{\Longrightarrow}&
  \text{every terminal triple violates \textsc{akr} Lemma 5} \\[4pt]
  &\Longrightarrow& \bot .
\end{array}
\]}

Hence $I\neq 40$; with $I\le42$ (\textsf{C1}), $I\neq42$ (\S\ref{sec:i42}) and
$I$ even, this gives $I\le 38$, and the explicit construction \textsf{LB38}
attains $38$. Therefore $f(7,7)=38$.

\begin{remark}
All of this is conditional on the general-position regime \textsf{GP}, imported
from \cite[\S9.1]{AckermanKeszeghRote2022}; see \S\ref{sec:gp}.
\end{remark}

\section{External mathematical dependencies}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textsf{GP} --- general position, AKR \S9}{GP -- general position, AKR S9}}\label{sec:gp}

\textbf{Role.} The whole combinatorial argument is carried out in a
general-position regime. \textsf{GP} is what licenses that restriction.

\textbf{Source.} \cite[\S9.1, and \S3]{AckermanKeszeghRote2022}. Their
perturbation removes the relevant degeneracies without decreasing the number of
boundary intersections.

\textbf{Exact consequences used, and nothing stronger.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(GP\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
  \item both polygons remain simple;
  \item no vertex of one polygon lies on the boundary of the other, so every
    intersection point is interior to two sides --- this is what allows
    intersections to be counted as proper crossings;
  \item no three vertices of $P$ and $Q$ combined are collinear;
  \item no two sides of $P$ and $Q$ combined are parallel;
  \item consequently the supporting line $\ell(q_j)$ of any side of $Q$ contains
    no vertex of $P$ (it passes through two vertices of $Q$, so a vertex of $P$
    on it would violate (GP3)). This is exactly the hypothesis that
    Lemma~A needs, and it is the \emph{only} kind of line at which Lemma~A is
    ever instantiated.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[A superseded project argument]
Earlier versions of this project proved \textsf{GP} by a project-specific
perturbation rule. That argument is \textbf{incorrect}: its rule for a
vertex-on-vertex degeneracy, ``push the coincident vertex into $\Cone_Q(w)$'',
destroys the intersection point whenever $\Cone_P(v)\subset\Cone_Q(w)$, yielding
$0$ crossings instead of $\ge1$. It also never addressed the case in which one
side participates in several degeneracies at once. It has been removed from the
active proof and replaced by the citation above. See \S\ref{sec:defects}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Counting model]
\cite[\S9.3]{AckermanKeszeghRote2022} explicitly leaves the variant counting
intersecting \emph{side pairs} rather than intersection \emph{points} as future
research. Everything here is in the intersection-\emph{point} model, and the
result must not be restated in the pairs model.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textsf{C1} --- {\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 1}{C1 -- Cerny Lemma 1}}\label{sec:c1}

A line meets a simple $k$-gon an even number of times, hence at most $k-1$ times
for odd $k$ \cite[Lemma 1]{CernyEtAl2003}. Applied to $\ell(p_i)$ against $Q$ and
to $\ell(q_j)$ against $P$, every side misses at least one side of the other
polygon. The single consequence used is
\[
  \delta(\GD)\ \ge\ 1 .
\]
Together with $|\ED|=49-I$ this also gives $I\le42$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textsf{C2} --- {\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 2}{C2 -- Cerny Lemma 2}}

The only consequence used is a local forbidden configuration. Let
$A,B,C,D,E$ be five consecutive vertices of $P$, giving four consecutive sides
$AB,BC,CD,DE$, and suppose $D\in\operatorname{int}\triangle ABC$. Then for no
four sides of $Q$ is the induced subgraph of $\GD$ on those four sides of $P$ and
those four sides of $Q$ \emph{exactly} a perfect matching
\cite[Lemma 2]{CernyEtAl2003}.

The geometric hypothesis depends only on the order type and is tabulated per
exceptional class as the \emph{Lemma-2 windows}. The word \emph{exactly} is
load-bearing: a block containing a perfect matching \emph{plus} an extra edge is
not the forbidden pattern \emph{for that block}. (It does not follow that the
graph survives the filter --- another block may still trigger it.)

\subsection{\textsf{A5} --- AKR Lemma 5}

\textbf{Statement used.} If two sides $p_a,p_b$ of $P$ that are \emph{consecutive
or avoiding} lie in different components of $\GD$, then there are consecutive
sides $q_i,q_{i\pm1}$ of $Q$ with
\[
  (p_a,q_i),\,(p_b,q_{i\pm1})\in\ED
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  (p_a,q_{i\pm1}),\,(p_b,q_i)\notin\ED .
\]

\textbf{\textsf{A5-C}, consecutive use.} Applied to every consecutive pair in
different components; this is the first filter of the pipeline.

\textbf{\textsf{A5-A}, avoiding use.} Two non-adjacent sides are \emph{avoiding}
when neither contains the intersection point of their supporting lines. This is an
order-type predicate, tabulated per exceptional class. \textsf{A5-A} is the final
filter and the one the certificate records.

\textbf{Combinatorial consequence.} Writing $N(\cdot)$ for neighbourhoods in
$\GD$, the displayed conclusion is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adj}
  \exists\, k\in N(p_a)\setminus N(p_b),\quad
  \exists\, k'\in N(p_b)\setminus N(p_a),\qquad
  k' \equiv k\pm 1 \pmod 7 .
\end{equation}
Both directions are immediate by unfolding: $k\in N(p_a)\setminus N(p_b)$ says
exactly $(p_a,q_k)\in\ED$ and $(p_b,q_k)\notin\ED$.

\begin{remark}
This equivalence is \textbf{Lean-kernel-checked}, as
\texttt{akr5Conclusion\_iff\_adjacent} in \texttt{lean/EndgameDefs.lean}, and
depends on no axioms. It matters because it shows the implemented predicate is
neither a weakening nor a strengthening of the published conclusion.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
If $\CC(p_a)\neq\CC(p_b)$ then $N(p_a)\cap N(p_b)=\emptyset$, since a shared
neighbour would join them in one component. The set differences in
\eqref{eq:adj} are then the neighbourhoods themselves.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textsf{A7-EXT} and \textsf{A7-BRIDGE} --- AKR Lemma 7 and bipartiteness of $H$}{A7-EXT and A7-BRIDGE -- AKR Lemma 7 and bipartiteness of H}}\label{sec:a7ext}

This dependency was previously recorded as a single item. It bundles two things
of very different character, and is split here:
\[
  \underbrace{\textsf{A7-EXT}}_{\text{published theorem}+\text{interpretation}}
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \underbrace{\textsf{A7-BRIDGE}}_{\text{project inference, Lean-checked}}
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  H \text{ bipartite}.
\]

\textbf{Definition of $H$.} Fix $P$ (symmetrically for $Q$). Vertices are the
relevant consecutive pairs, $V(H)=\{\,i : \CC(p_i)\neq\CC(p_{i+1})\,\}$, and
$i\sim_H k$ iff the two pairs share no side, i.e.\ $(k-i)\bmod 7\notin\{0,1,6\}$,
\emph{and} the four sides $p_i,p_{i+1},p_k,p_{k+1}$ lie in four distinct
components of $\GD$. Condition (b) is imposed directly, so an $H$-edge reproduces
Lemma 7's hypothesis exactly.

For $e\in V(H)$ let $A(e)$ be the set of pairs of consecutive sides of $Q$
associated to $e$ \emph{in the narrow sense} --- carrying, beyond the two edges
of $\ED$, the \emph{cone dichotomy} of Lemma 5 --- and put
\[
  S(e)=\{\text{hooking}: \exists\alpha\in A(e),\ I(\alpha)\in\Cone(e)\}
     \cup\{\text{hooked}: \exists\alpha\in A(e),\ I(e)\in\Cone(\alpha)\}.
\]
$S(e)\neq\emptyset$: $e\in V(H)$ is Lemma 5's hypothesis, so $A(e)\neq\emptyset$,
and Lemma 5's dichotomy realises at least one status.

\begin{remark}[Which extra conditions the narrow sense actually needs]\label{rem:narrow}
Lemma 5 delivers, beyond the two edges $(p_i,q_j),(p_{i+1},q_{j\pm1})\in\ED$,
both the two cross non-edges and the cone dichotomy. Only the second is a genuine
strengthening.

\emph{The cross non-edges are automatic.} If $(p_i,q_{j\pm1})\in\ED$ then
$p_i - q_{j\pm1} - p_{i+1}$ is a path in $\GD$, so $\CC(p_i)=\CC(p_{i+1})$,
contradicting $e\in V(H)$. This is pure connectivity; bipartiteness of $\GD$
plays no part. Checked exhaustively over all $2^{16}$ bipartite graphs on $4+4$
vertices: $110{,}304$ qualifying configurations, $0$ violations.

\emph{The cone dichotomy is not.} Adding the non-edges does \emph{not} recover
Observation 6: for
$a=[(0,0),(2,0)]$, $b=[(0,0),(0,3)]$, $A=[(-1,10),(\tfrac1{10},\tfrac65)]$,
$B=[(-1,10),(\tfrac75,-2)]$ all four edge and non-edge conditions hold with $a,b$
consecutive, yet the axis $y=-10x$ leaves both $a$ and $b$ in $y\ge-10x$.

So the narrow notion used here is \textbf{edges $+$ cone dichotomy}, the
non-edges being free. A formalisation that instead defined it as
``edges $+$ cross non-edges'' and then asserted Observation 6 would be unsound.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{\textsf{A7-EXT} --- external, \stat{very strongly corroborated}}

\begin{quote}
Under the intended interpretation of ``associated'' and ``is hooking'',
\cite[Lemma 7]{AckermanKeszeghRote2022} yields: if $e\sim_H e'$ then
$S(e)\cap S(e')=\emptyset$.
\end{quote}

This is the one item that remains subject to external human review, for three
reasons, in increasing order of comfort.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textsc{akr}'s Observation 6 (the Axis Property) is stated
    \emph{without proof} in both versions, and is \emph{false as printed}. The
    counterexample is not a bare four-segment picture but a pair of genuine
    simple polygons in full general position,
    \[
      P=(2,0),(3,4),(-2,0);\qquad Q=(4,-4),(-3,1),(0,-1),(3,3),(2,1),
    \]
    verified in exact integer arithmetic (\S\ref{sec:defects}, item 6). Here
    $q_0,q_4$ is associated to $p_0,p_1$ under the printed definition --- indeed
    the cross non-edges hold too --- yet the axis separates nothing. Observation 6
    is used throughout Lemma 7's case analysis.

    This does \emph{not} refute \textsf{A7-EXT}. Each status is by definition one
    disjunct of the cone dichotomy, so an associated pair failing the dichotomy
    contributes nothing to $S(e)$ and can never participate in the hypothesis.
    Equivalently, Lemma 7's hypothesis supplies the dichotomy exactly on the
    pairs that matter (Remark~\ref{rem:narrow}).
  \item The conference version \cite{AckermanKeszeghRote2020} opens its proof of
    Lemma 7 by \emph{selecting} the associated pairs subject to the cone
    conditions $I(q_{i'},q_{i'\pm1})\in\Cone(p_i,p_{i+1})$, which forces the
    existential reading of ``is hooking'' used above. The journal version deleted
    those clauses, leaving its own next sentence unsupported. This strongly
    indicates the intended reading but does not state it in one place.
  \item \cite[\S10.2]{AckermanKeszeghRote2022}, journal only, reports the
    authors' own independent computer verification of Lemma 7: $158{,}830$
    arrangements of eight lines, $840$ assignments each after symmetry, yielding
    $14$ configurations of which $13$ fail Lemma 5's hooking/hooked pattern. This
    corroborates the \emph{statement}. It also corroborates the
    \emph{interpretation}: the stated ground for discarding those $13$ is that
    ``some associated pair did not conform to the hooking/hooked pattern of
    Lemma 5'', a sentence that is coherent only if ``associated'' already carries
    the dichotomy.

    Two of the four published numbers in \S10.2 do not reproduce, neither
    affecting the conclusion. $158{,}830$ is a \emph{nine}-element count
    (abstract order types on $9$ points); $x$-monotone \textsc{psla}s of $8$
    pseudolines correspond to abstract order types on $9$ points, the ninth
    element being the line at infinity, so a complete but redundant superset was
    enumerated --- harmless for an impossibility claim. $840=8!/48$ quotients by
    $\operatorname{Aut}$ of the disjointness graph alone ($\cong Q_3$, order
    $48$), a group that does not preserve which pairs are consecutive; the
    stabiliser of the configuration has order $16$, giving $8!/16=2520$,
    reproduced by brute force over $S_8$. Both are fair questions for the
    authors.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Independent verification of \textsf{A7-EXT}}

An oriented-matroid (rank-3 chirotope) encoding over the twelve segment
endpoints makes the both-hooking and both-hooked patterns \textsc{unsat}, while
the mixed patterns of \textsc{akr}'s Figure 6 are \textsc{sat}. Unsatisfiability
over a \emph{superset} of realisable configurations is sound for an
impossibility claim, so under-constraining is safe and only over-constraining
would be fatal. The encoding was attacked on exactly that point:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item every encoded predicate was checked against the paper, and each
    constraint is a \emph{necessary} consequence of the hypothesis;
  \item \textbf{positive control:} \textsc{akr}'s own Figure 6 configuration,
    reconstructed from integer line coefficients in exact rationals, satisfies
    all $64{,}566$ clauses --- so the encoding is not vacuously over-constrained;
  \item an independent re-encoding from scratch reproduces the verdicts;
  \item re-solved with CaDiCaL, Glucose and MiniSat, all agreeing;
  \item \textsc{drat} refutations verified by a from-scratch
    \textsc{rup}/\textsc{rat} checker, itself validated on $\mathrm{PHP}(4)$ and
    $\mathrm{PHP}(5)$;
  \item the chirotope axiom set validated by reproducing the published
    enumeration counts $1,1,1,4$ for $n=3,\dots,6$ (\textsc{oeis} A006248);
  \item \textbf{case exhaustion, after a repair.} The original coverage argument
    reduced the adjacency cases by a symmetry orbit and was \emph{wrong}
    (\S\ref{sec:defects}, item~7); the \textsf{PQ-adj} verdicts it produced were
    vacuous. It is superseded by a derivation that uses no orbit argument at
    all: in exact $\mathbb{Z}_7$ arithmetic the coincidence equations give five
    types per polygon, hence $5\times5=25$ combined, confirmed by an exhaustive
    sweep of $50{,}176$ labelled cases with $0$ violations. All $25$ are then
    solved separately, with \textsf{both-hooking} and \textsf{both-hooked}
    \textsc{unsat} in every one. That is $125$ instances and $125$ certificates
    --- $110$ \textsc{drat} refutations and $15$ explicit models --- with no
    solver-only verdict, each certificate bound to its formula by \textsc{sha}-256
    and re-derived from source before checking.
\end{itemize}

Separately, a direct search for a counterexample on real heptagon pairs over
exact integers, with simplicity and full general position enforced, examined
${\sim}57{,}000$ admissible configurations and $306$ associated pairs on
consecutive pairs in different components: $0$ violations.

\begin{remark}[Why this is not \stat{verified independently}]\label{rem:a7gap}
The machine checks are certificates about a chirotope encoding. Four steps
connecting them to the geometric statement are hand-checked prose and none is
certified: (i) general position of the $\le12$ vertices is \textsc{wlog}
(inherited from \textsf{GP}; perturbation can change $\GD$); (ii) ``four distinct
components'' yields exactly the encoded pair pattern --- $24$ requirements
($16$ cross-polygon, $4$ within $P$, $4$ within $Q$), of which those sharing an
endpoint carry no expressible constraint; (iii) the enumerated case list
exhausts $n=7$ --- now derived exhaustively in exact arithmetic rather than by
the orbit argument that turned out to be wrong, but by a project script, not by
a certificate; (iv) Lemma 7's quantification matches the existential $S(e)$. The translation of ``segments cross'' and ``point in cone'' into
chirotope conditions is validated by exact random testing, not by proof. And
$S(e)\neq\emptyset$, needed alongside \textsf{A7-EXT}, rests on Lemma 5, which is
machine-checked nowhere.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{\textsf{A7-BRIDGE} --- project inference, \stat{lean-kernel-checked}}

\begin{quote}
If every relevant vertex has a non-empty status set, and adjacent vertices have
disjoint status sets, then $H$ is bipartite.
\end{quote}

\emph{Proof.} Let $e\sim_H e'$. Both $S(e),S(e')$ are non-empty and disjoint
subsets of a two-element set, hence distinct singletons. So if
$S(e)=\{\text{hooking},\text{hooked}\}$ then $e$ has no $H$-neighbour at all; every
non-isolated vertex has $|S(e)|=1$; every $H$-edge joins opposite singletons; and
$e\mapsto S(e)$ is a proper $2$-colouring of the non-isolated part, isolated
vertices being irrelevant to $2$-colourability. $\square$

This inference is formalised in \texttt{lean/}\allowbreak\texttt{Lemma7Bridge}\allowbreak\texttt{.lean}
(\S\ref{sec:lean}). The disjointness condition is an explicit \emph{hypothesis};
AKR Lemma 7 is not assumed, not axiomatised, and not named in the file.

\begin{remark}[What the Lean check does and does not buy]
It settles the ``different associated pairs witness different statuses'' worry
completely: a both-status vertex is \emph{proved} isolated, so the scenario in
which Lemma 7 is applied with inconsistent choices on different $H$-edges is
vacuous. It says nothing whatever about whether \textsc{akr}'s published language
licenses \textsf{A7-EXT}. Lean verifies the project's implication; it does not
verify \textsc{akr}'s geometric theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Project-original mathematical reductions}

Four items: Lemma~A and the exceptional classification, which feed the
enumeration; parity, which fixes the two branches to be excluded; and the
exclusion of $I=42$, which is one of them. Nothing here is imported.

\subsection{Lemma A}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma A]\label{lem:A}
Let $S\subseteq\operatorname{sides}(P)$ satisfy
\[
  (\star)\qquad
  \forall \ell,\ \ \ell\cap V(P)=\emptyset
  \ \Longrightarrow\
  \ell \text{ misses at least one member of } S .
\]
Then $|\ED| \ge n + (m-|S|)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For each side $q_j$ of $Q$, (GP5) gives $\ell(q_j)\cap V(P)=\emptyset$, so
$(\star)$ applies and yields $p\in S$ with $\ell(q_j)\cap p=\emptyset$, hence
$q_j\cap p=\emptyset$. This is $n$ edges with distinct $Q$-endpoints, all with
$P$-endpoint in $S$. By \textsf{C1} each $p\notin S$ misses some side of $Q$,
giving $m-|S|$ further edges with distinct $P$-endpoints outside $S$. The two
families are disjoint because their $P$-endpoints lie inside versus outside $S$.
\end{proof}

For $m=n=7$ and $|S|=4$ this gives $|\ED|\ge10$; since $I$ is even, $|\ED|=49-I$
is odd, so $|\ED|\ge11$ and $I\le38$. Call $P$ \emph{exceptional} when no
$4$-subset of its sides satisfies $(\star)$. Then
\[
  I=40 \ \Longrightarrow\ P \text{ and } Q \text{ are both exceptional}.
\]

\textbf{Lean boundary.} \texttt{lean/}\allowbreak\texttt{LemmaA}\allowbreak\texttt{.lean} kernel-checks the counting and
the parity upgrade (\texttt{card\_E\_ge}, \texttt{card\_E\_ge\_eleven},
\texttt{crossings\_le\_38}) as an \emph{implication}. Of the two geometric
premises:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item $h_2$ --- that each $p\notin S$ is disjoint from some $q_j$ --- is
    \emph{derived}, for an arbitrary configuration, by the parity argument of
    \texttt{SegmentGeometry.lean} (\textsf{LEAN-GEO}). It is no longer an
    assumption;
  \item $h_1$ --- that each $q_j$ is disjoint from some member of $S$ --- remains
    an explicit hypothesis, discharged in Lean only for the one certificate pair.
\end{itemize}
The $(\star)$ condition itself is not formalized. Separately,
\texttt{LemmaABridge.lean} connects Lemma~A's \texttt{Finset} cardinality to the
mask-level \texttt{degSum} used everywhere else, which is what lets the
$|\ED|<10\Rightarrow$ ``exceptional'' step be applied rather than assumed
(\textsf{X3}).

\subsection{Exceptional classification}\label{sec:exc}

All predicates used --- simplicity, crossing sets, the avoiding relation, the
Lemma-2 windows --- are Boolean functions of the order type of the seven
vertices. The classification is therefore finite:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  135 \text{ order types}\ \times\ 360 \text{ cyclic orders}
  &\ \longrightarrow\ 6{,}342 \text{ simple heptagons} \\
  &\ \longrightarrow\ 16 \text{ exceptional}
  \ \longrightarrow\ 14 \text{ classes}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The $6{,}342$ split as $6{,}007$ with an uncrossable $3$-set, $319$ with an
uncrossable $4$-set, and $16$ exceptional. Each of the $14$ classes has
$|M|=5$, exactly five maximal crossing sets all of size $6$, and two avoiding
pairs --- except one class with none.

\begin{remark}[Strict versus loose, and why it matters]\label{rem:strict}
Two readings of ``uncrossable'' must be distinguished.
\[
  \textbf{strict: } (\star) \text{ above}
  \qquad\text{versus}\qquad
  \textbf{loose: } \text{no line at all meets all four closed sides.}
\]
Measured over all $6{,}342$ simple heptagons these give
\[
  \textbf{strict} \rightsquigarrow 16 \text{ exceptional},
  \qquad
  \textbf{loose} \rightsquigarrow 2{,}268 \text{ exceptional},
\]
differing on $2{,}252$ objects. The \emph{strict} reading is the correct one:
Lemma~A is only ever instantiated at lines $\ell(q_j)$, which avoid $V(P)$ by
(GP5). The $2{,}252$ differing objects are exactly those $4$-sets whose only
transversals pass through a vertex of $P$ --- and no $\ell(q_j)$ can be such a
line. Had the loose phrasing been implemented, the finite search would have been
infeasible.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Parity: $I$ is even}{Parity: I is even}}\label{sec:par}

\textbf{Role.} With $I\le42$ from \textsf{C1}, parity is what reduces the upper
bound to the two branches $I=42$ and $I=40$. Without it, $39$ and $41$ would
also need excluding.

\textbf{Status.} \stat{lean-kernel-checked} as of 2026-09-14
(\texttt{IntersectionParity.crossCountC\_even}), for an \emph{arbitrary}
configuration rather than the certificate pair. It was previously carried as an
assumption. The three standard axioms only.

\textbf{The argument.} Two closed curves in general position cross an even
number of times. Formalised so as to stay inside $\mathbb{Z}$, introducing no
reals and so preserving the Mathlib-free trust base of the geometry group:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item an $\mathrm{inside}$ predicate for a point with respect to $\partial Q$,
        defined by \emph{ray-crossing parity} under a sheared height order --- the
        lexicographic $(y,x)$ order, which \texttt{hlt\_eq\_decide} identifies
        with the order of the integer functional $p\mapsto N p_2+p_1$ once $N$
        exceeds the $x$-spread, i.e.\ a ray obtained from the horizontal by an
        infinitesimal tilt;
  \item a four-point bridge lemma relating the orientation predicates of the two
        segment endpoints to that parity;
  \item the per-side lemma \texttt{crossRow\_parity}: side $i$ of $P$ crosses
        $\partial Q$ an odd number of times exactly when its two endpoints differ
        in inside-ness;
  \item the cyclic sum: each vertex indicator occurs in exactly two consecutive
        sides, so summing (3) around the closed polygon doubles every term. This
        is the same cancellation \texttt{SegmentGeometry.cycChanges\_even}
        performs for a line.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Not the same as the line parity already present]
\texttt{SegmentGeometry.metCount\_even} --- a closed polygon meets a \emph{line}
an even number of times --- is a different statement, and is what feeds
\textsf{C1} and hence $\delta(\GD)\ge1$. It does not give \textsf{PAR}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textsf{UB42} --- excluding $I=42$}{UB42 -- excluding I = 42}}\label{sec:i42}

\textbf{Role.} \textsf{C1} caps $I$ at $42$, not at $40$, so the value $42$ must
be excluded on its own. The manifest previously asserted twice that it is
``excluded separately'' without giving the argument. It is this.

\textbf{Status.} \stat{lean-kernel-checked} as
\texttt{Exclude42.not\_crossCount\_42}, given the two named inputs \textsf{X1}
and \textsf{X2}; see \S\ref{sec:lean}. Also verified by exhaustive sweep, below.

$I=42$ forces $|\ED|=7$. With $\delta(\GD)\ge1$ on $7+7$ vertices and only $7$
edges, every vertex has degree exactly $1$, so $\GD$ is a \emph{perfect
matching}. Its components are therefore the $7$ matched pairs: each component
contains exactly one side of $P$ and one of $Q$. In particular no two sides of
$P$ share a component.

Next, both polygons are \emph{exceptional}. Lemma~\ref{lem:A} with $|S|=4$ would
force $|\ED|\ge 7+(7-4)=10>7$, so neither $P$ nor $Q$ admits an uncrossable
$4$-set. Hence the crossing-set data of \S\ref{sec:exc} applies to each.

Now fix $j$. Since $\GD$ is a perfect matching, $N(q_j)=\{i_j\}$ is a singleton,
so the set of sides of $P$ that $q_j$ \emph{crosses} is
$\{0,\dots,6\}\setminus\{i_j\}$, of size $6$. Compatibility requires this
crossing set to be contained in a maximal crossing set of $P$'s class. Every
exceptional class has exactly five maximal crossing sets, each of size $6$ and of
the form $\{0,\dots,6\}\setminus\{m\}$ for $m\in M_P$, with $|M_P|=5$. Two
$6$-subsets of a $7$-set nest only if they are equal, so $i_j\in M_P$. This holds
for every $j$, and the $i_j$ are distinct because $\GD$ is a matching, so all
seven sides of $P$ lie in $M_P$ --- contradicting $|M_P|=5$. Hence $I\neq42$.

\begin{remark}
Verified by exhaustive sweep over all $7!=5{,}040$ perfect matchings against all
$14$ profile classes: $0$ survive. Re-run against the independent profile
reconstruction of \S\ref{sec:impl} (whose class labels differ from the shipped
ones by a dihedral relabelling of side indices, matched $14/14$): also $0$. The
two invariants consumed --- $|M|=5$, and maximal crossing sets
$=\{\textsc{full}\setminus\{m\}: m\in M\}$ --- were recomputed from the class data
rather than read from the stored invariant block, and hold for all $14$ classes
in both reconstructions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[This branch does not depend on \textsf{A7-EXT}]\label{rem:i42noa7}
Earlier versions of this manifest excluded $I=42$ through \textsf{A7-EXT}: a
perfect matching makes every consecutive pair relevant and every index-non-adjacent
pair span four distinct components, so $H$ is the complement of the $7$-cycle,
which contains the triangle $e_0e_2e_4$ and is not bipartite. That argument is
correct but unnecessary. The route above consumes only \textsf{C1},
\textsf{LA} and the exceptional-class invariants \textsf{EX}, all of which are
elementary and order-type based, so no further external lemma is imported ---
in particular not \cite[Lemma 3]{CernyEtAl2003}, whose four-page case analysis
this project has not audited and which an earlier draft wrongly treated as the
only available reroute.

The practical consequence is that \textsf{A7-EXT} is load-bearing at exactly
\emph{one} point in the proof: the $I=40$ endgame. It cannot be dropped there ---
with the filter off the pipeline ends at $2{,}187$ surviving triples on $412$
graphs, not $0$ (\S\ref{sec:comp}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The shipped $|\ED|=7$ sweep reproduces the older route independently:
$5{,}040 \to 14$ (\textsf{A5-C}) $\to 0$ (\textsf{A7-EXT}). The companion
$|\ED|=8$ sweep gives $476{,}280\to1{,}078\to0$, for $I=41$, which parity already
excludes. Both were a by-product of the historical enumeration bug of
Remark~\ref{rem:bug} and are corroborative rather than load-bearing.
\end{remark}

\section{Computational reduction}\label{sec:comp}

\subsection{The universe of candidate graphs}

There are $7\times 7 = 49$ side pairs, and $I+|\ED|=49$, so $I=40$ forces
$|\ED|=9$. By \textsf{C1} the minimum degree is at least $1$ on both sides. The
number of bipartite graphs on $7+7$ vertices with exactly $9$ edges and minimum
degree at least $1$ is
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{7}(-1)^j\binom{7}{j}\,[x^9]\bigl((1+x)^{7-j}-1\bigr)^{7}
  \;=\; 13{,}003{,}620 ,
\]
by inclusion--exclusion over uncovered $Q$-sides. This closed form was evaluated
in exact integer arithmetic independently of both enumerations.

\begin{remark}[A historical enumeration bug]\label{rem:bug}
An earlier implementation reported $13{,}484{,}940$ as ``the number of $9$-edge
graphs''. Its recursion never forced the edge budget to be exhausted at the leaf,
so it enumerated $|\ED|\in\{7,8,9\}$:
\[
  5{,}040 \;+\; 476{,}280 \;+\; 13{,}003{,}620 \;=\; 13{,}484{,}940 .
\]
The searched universe was therefore a \emph{superset} of what was needed. No
candidate was missed and the exclusion was not invalidated --- only the reported
figure was wrong, and the $|\ED|=7$ and $|\ED|=8$ cases were swept for free. The
bug nonetheless motivated the independent reimplementation of \S\ref{sec:impl}.

\begin{remark}[The $121\to225$ step is not a filter]\label{rem:121}
This is a change in what is being counted, and it should not be read as a
reduction. The figures $13{,}003{,}620$ through $121$ count \emph{graphs}; the
figure $225$ counts \emph{triples} $(\text{class}_P,\text{class}_Q,G)$. The $225$
triples live on exactly those same $121$ graphs: a graph may be compatible with
several exceptional classes on either side, and $225=\sum_G |ok_P(G)|\cdot|ok_Q(G)|$
over the $121$ surviving graphs. Every one of the $121$ graphs has degree sequence
$(2,2,1,1,1,1,1)$ on both sides, and only $9$ of the $14$ classes occur.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\textsf{A7-EXT} cannot be dropped from this branch]\label{rem:noa7}
The filter at $42{,}238$ is the only place \textsf{A7-EXT} enters the proof
(Remark~\ref{rem:i42noa7}), which raises the question of whether it is needed at
all. It is. Re-running the whole pipeline with that filter switched off gives
\[
  13{,}003{,}620 \to 42{,}238 \to 6{,}376 \to 533 \to 2{,}412 \to \mathbf{2{,}187}
\]
surviving triples, on $412$ graphs --- not $0$. The structure is sharp: the $533$
graphs surviving {\v C}ern{\'y} L2 without \textsf{A7-EXT} split as exactly
$121$ $H$-bipartite $+$ $412$ non-bipartite; the $121$ are identical to the
shipped certificate's graphs; and the avoiding-pair filter \textsf{A5-A} kills
all $121$ and \emph{none} of the $412$. On the residual the two filters are
perfectly complementary, so no reordering helps. No already-trusted ingredient
substitutes: compatibility is already applied at full strength, and
{\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 3 kills $0$ of the $412$.

This is evidence about the pipeline, not about \textsc{akr} Lemma 7: it shows the
present toolkit cannot avoid \textsf{A7-EXT}, not that \textsf{A7-EXT} is
mathematically indispensable.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[{\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 3 is not a dependency]
An earlier pipeline also applied {\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 3 between \textsf{A7-EXT} and
the compatibility filter, reducing $24{,}990$ to $9{,}702$. Running the pipeline
with and without it produces \emph{identical} results at every subsequent stage
and an identical set of $225$ terminal triples: the graphs Lemma 3 removes are
all removed by crossing-set compatibility in any case. Lemma 3 is therefore
\emph{not} a logical dependency of this proof and is omitted throughout.
\end{remark}

\textbf{C++17 (independent reduction).} Written from a written specification,
with every major algorithm chosen differently: enumeration transposed to
$Q$-side columns and partitioned by an explicit degree sequence rather than an
edge budget; graphs keyed as a packed $49$-bit word; components by iterative
bitmask closure; bipartiteness by union-find \emph{with parity}; compatibility by
direct containment in the five maximal crossing sets; {\v C}ern{\'y} L2 by
enumeration of systems of distinct representatives.

\textbf{Agreement.} Both produce the same universe $13{,}003{,}620$; the same
intermediate counts $42{,}238$, $24{,}990$, $2{,}300$, $121$; the same $121$
graphs; and the same canonical set of $225$ terminal triples, with
\emph{zero set difference} in either direction.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the independence claim]
This is \textbf{implementation-independent in algorithm and data representation,
but not fully author- or specification-independent}: the second implementation
was written by an agent that had already seen the first implementation and its
specification. The agreement is therefore strong evidence against transcription
and coding errors, and against a missed branch in either enumeration, but it is
\emph{not} evidence against a shared misreading of the published lemmas.
\end{remark}

\section{Certificates}\label{sec:cert}

A \emph{certificate} here is a file that lets a referee re-derive the terminal
contradictions without trusting --- or rerunning --- the exhaustive search. For
each of the $225$ terminal triples it stores the class identifiers, a canonical
graph encoding (seven neighbourhood bitmasks), the degree sequences, the
component partitions, the relevant side pair and its kind, both neighbourhoods,
the two set differences, and the list of cyclic adjacencies found, which is empty
in every case.

The standalone checker recomputes every quantity from the canonical encoding:
neighbourhoods, degree sequences, components (by breadth-first search, where the
generator used union-find), the set differences, and the adjacency test. The
certificate's convenience fields are treated as claims to be checked, never as
input.

\begin{remark}[Component labels are compared modulo relabelling]
Component identifiers are arbitrary. The checker compares the induced
\emph{partitions}, not the integer labels. An earlier version of the comparison
compared raw labels and reported spurious failures on $217$ of $225$ cases; the
check was corrected to be relabelling-invariant.
\end{remark}

\textbf{Adversarial controls.} The checker was validated against ten deliberate
corruptions, all detected: a case marked as surviving, a pair not avoiding for
its class, falsified neighbourhoods, falsified difference sets, a falsified
adjacency list, a graph edited to break the edge count or minimum degree, a wrong
degree sequence, a wrong component partition, and --- the essential control --- an
\emph{honestly constructed} witness using a different avoiding pair of the same
class for which Lemma 5 genuinely \emph{is} satisfied. That last case is rejected
with a message naming the satisfying adjacency, which confirms the checker does
not simply reject everything.

\subsection*{A worked terminal case}

Take the first certificate entry. The graph is given by the $P$-side
neighbourhoods
\[
  N(p_0),\dots,N(p_6)
  \;=\;
  \{0\},\{0\},\{1\},\{2,4\},\{3\},\{4\},\{5,6\},
\]
with $P$ assigned exceptional class $12$, whose $M$-set is $\{0,2,3,4,6\}$ and
whose avoiding pairs are $(0,2)$ and $(4,6)$. The sides $p_4$ and $p_6$ are
avoiding and lie in different components. Now
\[
  N(p_4)=\{3\},\qquad N(p_6)=\{5,6\},
\]
so $N(p_4)\setminus N(p_6)=\{3\}$ and $N(p_6)\setminus N(p_4)=\{5,6\}$. In
$\mathbb{Z}_7$ the element $3$ is cyclically adjacent only to $2$ and $4$, and
neither lies in $\{5,6\}$. So \eqref{eq:adj} fails, contradicting \textsf{A5-A}.
All $225$ cases have this shape.

For contrast, the \emph{other} avoiding pair $(0,2)$ of the same class on the same
graph gives $N(p_0)\setminus N(p_2)=\{0\}$ and $N(p_2)\setminus N(p_0)=\{1\}$,
and $0$ \emph{is} adjacent to $1$ --- so \eqref{eq:adj} holds there. The
condition is genuinely discriminating: across the $225$ certificate graphs,
$2{,}016$ of the $4{,}725$ side pairs satisfy it.

\subsection*{\texttt{CernyL2Interface.lean} --- \textsf{C2-BRIDGE}}
\textbf{Imports: none.} \textbf{Axioms:} the three standard ones; the pure
\texttt{decide} facts are \texttt{propext}-only.

Proves \texttt{l2FiresB\_iff}: the \emph{Lean transcription} of the filter fires iff an \emph{exact

$\mathrm{Edge}\,(s_t)\,(q_u)\leftrightarrow t=u$ carrying both halves --- all four

\subsection*{\texttt{DisjointnessGraph.lean} --- \textsf{I2a}}
\textbf{Imports:} \texttt{GeometryDefs}, \texttt{LowerBound},
\texttt{EndgameDefs}, \texttt{SegmentGeometry} (all Mathlib-free).
\textbf{Axioms:} \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound}.

The geometry-to-endgame bridge. \texttt{Config} packages two simple heptagons in
general position; \texttt{toMasks\_spec} proves that bit $j$ of mask $i$ holds
\emph{iff} side $i$ of $P$ does not properly cross side $j$ of $Q$, and
\texttt{min\_degree\_both\_sides} supplies minimum degree $\ge 1$ on both sides
by \emph{application} of the general theorem, not by \texttt{decide} on the
certificate.

\begin{remark}[Why the interface is split into \textsf{I2a} and \textsf{I2b}]
The interface as originally written stated \texttt{toMasks\_spec} against
\emph{point-set} disjointness of two segments in $\mathbb{R}^2$. That is not
statable in a Mathlib-free development: \texttt{Pt} is
$\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ and \texttt{side}\,$P\,i$ is never defined as a
set. Stating it needs Mathlib's \texttt{segment} and the reals. The interface
was therefore split: \textsf{I2a}, the orientation-level form proved here and
consumed by everything downstream, and \textsf{I2b}, the single point-set lemma
$\texttt{DisjointO}\;K\,i\,j \iff \operatorname{Disjoint}(\mathrm{seg}_P\,i,\mathrm{seg}_Q\,j)$,
which is quarantined in one leaf library so that the rest of the development
keeps its Mathlib-free base. \texttt{Endgame.lean} consumes only the
\emph{function} \texttt{toMasks}, never the specification, so no endgame theorem
depends on either half.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{\texttt{PointSetSemantics.lean} --- \textsf{I2b}}
\textbf{Imports:} \texttt{DisjointnessGraph} and Mathlib
(\texttt{Analysis.Convex.Segment}, \texttt{Data.Real.Basic}).
\textbf{Axioms:} the three standard ones.

\stat{lean-kernel-checked} as of 2026-09-14, and this closes \textsf{I2b}. The
integer plane is embedded coordinatewise in $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$ and
\texttt{disjointO\_iff\_disjoint} proves, \emph{in both directions and with no
hypotheses beyond \texttt{Config}'s own general-position fields},
\[
  \texttt{DisjointO}\;K\,i\,j
  \iff
  \operatorname{Disjoint}\bigl(\texttt{segment}_{\mathbb{R}}\,p_i,\ \texttt{segment}_{\mathbb{R}}\,q_j\bigr).
\]
Composed with \textsf{I2a} this gives \texttt{toMasks\_pointSet\_spec}: the
stored mask bit means what it is supposed to mean about point sets in the real
plane. Non-vacuity is proved on the certificate pair in both directions --- one
disjoint pair, and one properly crossing pair together with an explicit common
real point.

\textbf{What this does \emph{not} do.} It makes \emph{side} disjointness
faithful to $\mathbb{R}^2$. It says nothing about the polygon as a curve: the
Jordan-curve step --- that the closed polygonal chain of \texttt{Simple7} bounds
a region, which is what ``simple polygon'' means in $f(7,7)$ --- is still not
formalised anywhere, and neither is the general-position reduction \textsf{GP}.
Those are the two steps of \S\ref{sec:leanmain}, and this library reduces
neither.

\subsection*{The enumeration obligations --- \texttt{EnumerationDomain}, \texttt{DomainCount}, \texttt{EnumerationComplete}, \texttt{CounterexampleCount}}\label{sec:enumobl}
\textbf{Imports:} \texttt{DisjointnessGraph}, \texttt{Endgame}, and Mathlib
(\texttt{BigOperators}, \texttt{Polynomial.Coeff},
\texttt{InclusionExclusion}, \texttt{Nat.Choose.Sum},
\texttt{Finset.Powerset}, \texttt{Fintype.Pi}).
\textbf{Axioms:} \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound} throughout
\texttt{EnumerationDomain}'s Part~A --- \emph{no} \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
no Mathlib result is used in substance there; the counting parts add
\texttt{Classical.choice} through Mathlib's \texttt{Finset} and
\texttt{Polynomial} machinery.

``Completeness of the enumeration'' is four claims, not one:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item a hypothetical counterexample produces a graph satisfying the search
        conditions;
  \item the abstract search domain has $13{,}003{,}620$ elements;
  \item the search \emph{actually visits} every element of that domain;
  \item every valid configuration survives each filter until it reaches a
        terminal case proved impossible.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{(1) is proved, in full.} \texttt{toMasks\_inDomain\_of\_crossCount\_eq\_40}
states that for an \emph{arbitrary} \texttt{Config} --- not the certificate pair
--- $\texttt{crossCountC}\,K = 40$ implies $\texttt{InDomain}\,(\texttt{toMasks}\,K)$:
seven $7$-bit masks, nine edges, minimum degree $\ge 1$ on both sides. The route
is \texttt{crossCountC\_add\_degSum} (that is, $I+|\ED|=49$ proved for
\emph{every} configuration rather than checked on one) together with
\textsf{I2a}'s minimum-degree result. The hypothesis $I=40$ is an \emph{input};
why a counterexample must have exactly $40$ crossings is Lemma~A plus parity,
argued elsewhere. Definitionally, \texttt{degSum\_eq\_edgeCount} holds by
\texttt{rfl}, so \texttt{InDomain}'s edge clause \emph{is} the quantity the
certificate checker recomputes.

\textbf{(2) is proved.} \texttt{DomainCount.domainFinset\_card} closes it:
\[
  |\text{domainFinset}| = 13{,}003{,}620 ,
\]
supplying the two steps \texttt{EnumerationDomain.lean} had left open --- that
the $t$-th inclusion--exclusion term depends only on $|t|$ (the source of the
$\binom{7}{j}$), and that for $|t|=j$ it equals
$[x^9]\bigl((1+x)^{7-j}-1\bigr)^{7}$. \texttt{CounterexampleCount} then joins
(1) and (2) into the single sentence \emph{a hypothetical counterexample is one
of exactly $13{,}003{,}620$ mask functions}. The count is proved, not evaluated:
nothing in the kernel enumerates the set.

\textbf{(3) is proved, about the transcription.}
\texttt{EnumerationComplete.enumerate\_complete} proves, by induction on the
recursion depth, that the Lean transcription of
\texttt{python/elimination/audit\_enum.py}'s \texttt{rec} emits every element of
\texttt{InDomain} --- no bound, no special case --- and
\texttt{enumerate\_sound} proves the converse, so the recursion visits
\emph{exactly} the domain. The pruning bound $\min(3,\texttt{budget}-\texttt{rem})$
is transcribed verbatim and then \emph{proved harmless} rather than dropped. The
one presentational change is that the loop counter counts down, which makes the
recursion structural: no \texttt{partial}, no well-founded recursion, no
\texttt{decide} on the output list.

\textbf{(4) is stated, not proved; and none of this is about the shipped
programs.} Obligation (4) is \texttt{FilterSoundness.lean}, whose eight external
inputs are tabulated in \S\ref{sec:leaniface}; six of the eight are open.
Independently of that, obligations (3) and (4) alike are statements about
\emph{Lean transcriptions} written by hand from the Python and C++ sources.
Nothing in Lean reads, compiles, or cross-checks either program; there is no
extraction. So ``the search recursion is complete'' means ``the Lean function is
complete, and it was transcribed by eye from the Python''.

\begin{remark}[A degree-sequence question, raised and closed]

\textbf{\textsf{E6}, discharged.} \texttt{ComponentLabelling.lean} defines a
concrete labelling of the $14$-vertex disjointness graph by a fuel-bounded
closure iteration --- no \texttt{partial}, no well-founded recursion --- and
proves \emph{equal labels iff connected}, in both directions. The asymmetry the

``standard algorithmic fact'' \textsf{E8} named is now a kernel-checked theorem.

and the Python a DFS, whereas the verified Lean procedure is a fixed-point
colour propagation. It is in the same family, not a byte-for-byte transcription,
and no Lean code reads either program.

\begin{remark}[Transcription, not extraction --- the gap common to every filter]
\texttt{l5TestB}, \texttt{HAdj}, \texttt{CompatOk}, \texttt{L2Ok} and

\texttt{python/elimination/audit\_enum.py} and

development says ``the filter'', read ``the Lean transcription of the filter''.
This applies to \textsf{C2-BRIDGE}, to \textsf{E8}, to obligation (3), and to
every filter-soundness statement; it is a single systematic gap, not several
independent ones, and closing it would require extraction or a verified
compiler, neither of which is present.
\end{remark}

\texttt{audit\_enum.py} runs a \emph{sixth} filter, {\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 3,

\texttt{reduction.cpp} does not implement filter (e) at all

check is filters (a)--(d).

\subsection*{\texttt{Exclude42} and \textsf{X1}--\textsf{X3}}
\textbf{Imports:} \texttt{EnumerationDomain}, \texttt{FilterSoundness}, Mathlib.
\textbf{Axioms:} the three standard ones.

\texttt{Exclude42.lean} formalises \S\ref{sec:i42}. Kernel-checked from its
hypotheses: seven edges plus minimum degree $1$ forces every degree to be
exactly $1$ on either side (via the transpose double-count); a degree-$1$ mask
has at most one set bit; the partner function $j\mapsto i_j$ is well defined and
injective; and the nesting step, that a $6$-set $\{i:i\neq i_j\}$ inside a
$6$-set $\{i:i\neq m\}$ forces $m=i_j$. The corollary
\texttt{not\_crossCount\_42} is stated at the level of a geometric
\texttt{Config}, and $I+|\ED|=49$ and $\delta(\GD)\ge1$ are \emph{not}
hypotheses of it --- they are imported theorems.

Three external inputs were named. Two remain:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textsf{X1}, the exceptional-class invariant: $|M_P|=5$ and the maximal
    crossing sets are exactly $\{\textsc{full}\setminus\{m\}:m\in M_P\}$. This
    is part of \textsf{EX}, and it is \emph{not} proved in Lean.
  \item \textsf{X2}, the order-type fact behind filter (c) --- identical to
    \textsf{E4}, reused verbatim rather than restated.
  \item \textsf{X3}, that $P$ is exceptional, \stat{discharged} 2026-09-14 by
    \texttt{LemmaABridge.lean}: \texttt{card\_E\_eq\_degSum} identifies Lemma~A's
    \texttt{Finset} count with the mask-level \texttt{degSum}, after which
    $\texttt{degSum}<10$ yields ``no uncrossable $4$-set'' by application of
    Lemma~A rather than by assumption.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{\texttt{IntersectionParity}, \texttt{UpperBoundAssembly}, \texttt{MainTheorem} --- the assembly}
\textbf{Axioms:} the three standard ones throughout.

\texttt{UpperBoundAssembly} proves $I\le42$ \emph{unconditionally} for every
\texttt{Config}, by applying $I+|\ED|=49$ and the minimum-degree theorem --- so
the project's use of {\v C}ern{\'y} L1 is, at this point, a consequence of its
own parity argument rather than an import. It then proves that parity,
$I\neq42$ and $I\neq40$ suffice for $I\le38$, and supplies a variant in which
the $I=42$ branch is discharged through \texttt{Exclude42} instead of assumed.
\texttt{MainTheorem} writes down the target sentence; \texttt{IntersectionParity}
discharges parity (\textsf{PAR}, \S\ref{sec:par}) and assembles
\texttt{isGreatest\_38\_final}, quoted in \S\ref{sec:leanmain}.

Non-vacuity is proved rather than asserted at each stage: the certificate pair
satisfies parity and both exclusions, and the bound is attained there, so the
assembly is not a statement about the empty set and $38$ is not slack.

\begin{remark}[The one thing \texttt{hclass} is not known to be]
\texttt{hclass} asserts the exceptional-class data for \emph{every}
\texttt{Config}, not only for the ones the reductio reaches. That is stronger
than the mathematical claim it stands for, which is conditioned on $I=40$ (or,
in \texttt{Exclude42}, on $I=42$). Nothing in the development proves
\texttt{hclass} \emph{satisfiable}, and no non-vacuity witness for it is
supplied. A reader should therefore treat \texttt{isGreatest\_38\_final} as a
statement of the residue, not as evidence that the residue is consistent.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{\textsf{E5} --- attempted and \textsc{blocked}}\label{sec:e5}

\texttt{CernyL2Derivation.lean} is an \emph{attempt} at \textsf{E5},
{\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 2's consequence, and it does not succeed. Its own header
says so: ``this file does not discharge E5''. It must not be quoted as a proof
of \textsf{E5} or of {\v C}ern{\'y} Lemma 2.

What it proves is strictly weaker than \textsf{E5}: an integer affine identity
for the triangle determinant; that a positively oriented interior point stays in
any strict positive half-plane containing the triangle's vertices; that
simplicity excludes proper crossings even of adjacent sides; and the conditional
statement ``\textsf{E5} holds if \texttt{CollisionObstruction} does'', for the
full forward-or-reverse window predicate. \texttt{CollisionObstruction} is the
unresolved step, stated explicitly rather than assumed silently: the four
complementary crossing patterns at an inside-triangle window must force a
crossing between two sides of $Q$. That is a \emph{simultaneous} segment
realizability argument, and testing each selected segment separately cannot
reach it --- the file includes \texttt{localFixture}, a concrete configuration in
which all sixteen required crossing and non-crossing entries hold, the triangle
condition holds, the five-vertex path is simple, and cross general position
holds, yet the selected segments cross each other. It is deliberately not a
\texttt{Config}.

\subsection*{\textsf{UB40} is out of reach of the kernel}\label{sec:hne40}

\texttt{hne40} --- $I\neq40$ --- is the one hypothesis of
\texttt{isGreatest\_38\_final} that is not an external lemma but a
\emph{computation}. The natural hope is that the Lean kernel could simply
perform it. \textbf{It cannot}, and as of 2026-09-14 this is a settled negative,
not an open engineering question. \texttt{native\_decide} is excluded throughout
the project because it adds \texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool} to the trust base; what
follows concerns ordinary kernel reduction. Five routes were measured.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Monolithic kernel evaluation --- dead on memory.} A full
    traversal is $226{,}668{,}772$ recursive calls. A ladder of partial runs fits
    $\text{user} \approx 0.135 + 2.385\times10^{-4}\cdot\text{calls}$ seconds,
    i.e.\ ${\approx}15$ CPU-hours, but peak resident set runs at
    $20$--$25$\,KB per call: $4.5$--$5.8$\,TB against $8$\,GB installed. And that
    is for \emph{counting only}, with no filter evaluated.
  \item \textbf{Chunked evaluation --- memory solved, wrong problem.} Splitting
    by the first four rows holds memory near $2$\,GB
    ($1{,}917{,}400$ calls at $2.23$\,GB), which retires the $4.5$\,TB figure as
    an artifact of building one enormous term. But it does not help: the
    counting recursion memoises $226{,}668{,}772$ states down to $1{,}925$
    distinct ones, a sharing factor of ${\approx}117{,}750$, and counting was
    \emph{already} proved by \texttt{DomainCount}. Filter evaluation consumes the
    whole graph, so none of that sharing applies.
  \item \textbf{Per-graph filter cost --- the decisive measurement.} Forcing the
    kernel to evaluate the component labelling and bipartiteness test on
    $1$, $10$ and $40$ graphs gives a marginal cost of ${\approx}1.03$ CPU-seconds
    per graph. Over $13{,}003{,}620$ graphs that is ${\approx}3{,}700$ CPU-hours
    --- about $154$ days --- for \emph{two} of the five filters. The
    kernel-to-Python ratio is ${\approx}70{,}000$, not the ${\approx}500$ one
    would assume from arithmetic benchmarks. The obstacle is time, not memory.
  \item \textbf{Re-encoding the filters for speed --- $7.3\times$, need
    ${\approx}75\times$.} Rewriting the labelling as unrolled \texttt{Nat}
    bitmask arithmetic with a $14$-step fixed point (correctness checked against
    a reference first) takes $0.309$ down to $0.042$ CPU-seconds per graph on the
    same $40$-graph benchmark. Projected over all five filters that is
    ${\approx}1{,}000$--$1{,}500$ CPU-hours; a feasible one-off needs about $50$.
    The route was closed against a gate fixed in advance.
  \item \textbf{Structural shortcuts --- dead on arithmetic.} A
    \textsf{UB42}-style argument from cheap invariants cuts about $33\%$. Leading
    with the local, dynamic-programmable filters (crossing-set compatibility and
    {\v C}ern{\'y} L2) still leaves $2{,}783{,}508$ graphs of the $13$ million for
    some class pair. Neither is within two orders of magnitude of enough.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
\textbf{What is \emph{not} settled by this.} These are measurements about
evaluating the \emph{present} $13{,}003{,}620$-element domain in the kernel. A
mathematical restructuring that shrinks the domain itself would change the
picture entirely --- but that is mathematics, not engineering, and nobody has
one. The honest summary is that \textsf{UB40} will not become a Lean theorem by
computation on this architecture, and that if it is to be formalised at all it
must first be made smaller.

\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{Key finite and combinatorial components are
formally verified in Lean; the published geometric lemmas themselves remain

--- the strongest formalised statement carries the hypotheses \texttt{hclass}
and \texttt{hne40} (\S\ref{sec:leanmain}) --- the completeness of the $225$-case
list is not formalized, six of the eight external inputs
\textsf{E1}--\textsf{E8} are open, and every filter statement is about a

\end{center}

\section{Known defects found during development}\label{sec:defects}

Listed because they calibrate how much checking this argument has had.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Enumeration bug ($13{,}484{,}940$).} The leaf test omitted the
    edge-budget condition, so $|\ED|\in\{7,8,9\}$ was enumerated.
    \emph{Effect on the theorem:} none --- a superset was searched, so no
    candidate was missed. \emph{Repair:} corrected leaf test; the buggy file is
    preserved unpatched as evidence. \emph{Superseded by:} the corrected
    enumeration and the closed form of \S\ref{sec:comp}.
  \item \textbf{Raw component-ID comparison.} A verification script compared
    component \emph{labels} rather than the partitions they induce, and reported
    failures on $217$ of $225$ cases. \emph{Effect:} none on the proof; it was a
    checker bug. \emph{Repair:} compare partitions. \emph{Superseded by:} the
    current relabelling-invariant checker.
  \item \textbf{Lean \texttt{Odd} / \texttt{autoImplicit} defect.} In the earlier
    \texttt{LemmaA.lean} skeleton, \texttt{Odd} was out of scope and became a
    free implicit variable, so two theorems elaborated as vacuous statements with
    no error. \emph{Effect:} none, because the file had never been compiled.
    \emph{Repair:} rewritten with \texttt{autoImplicit false}.
    \emph{Superseded by:} the current \texttt{LemmaA.lean}.
  \item \textbf{Step 0 perturbation argument.} The project's own
    general-position proof is incorrect when $\Cone_P(v)\subset\Cone_Q(w)$, where
    its rule destroys the intersection point. \emph{Effect on the theorem:} none,
    because the required statement follows from
    \cite[\S9.1]{AckermanKeszeghRote2022}. \emph{Repair:} removed from the active
    proof. \emph{Superseded by:} \textsf{GP}, \S\ref{sec:gp}.
  \item \textbf{Strict versus loose exceptional condition.} Early phrasing said
    ``no line meets all four sides'', which does not license what the computation
    tests. \emph{Effect if it had been implemented:} severe --- $2{,}268$
    exceptional objects instead of $16$, and an infeasible search.
    \emph{Repair:} the strict, vertex-avoiding form $(\star)$.
    \emph{Superseded by:} Lemma~\ref{lem:A} and Remark~\ref{rem:strict}.
  \item \textbf{\textsc{akr} Observation 6 is false as printed} --- a defect in a
    published paper, not in this project. Stated without proof in both versions
    and used throughout the case analysis of Lemma 7. The counterexample is a
    pair of genuine simple polygons,
    \[
      P=(2,0),(3,4),(-2,0);\qquad Q=(4,-4),(-3,1),(0,-1),(3,3),(2,1),
    \]
    verified in exact integer arithmetic and independently re-verified. Both are
    simple; the eight vertices are distinct; no three of the eight are collinear;
    no vertex of one lies on the other; no two of the eight sides are parallel;
    $I(P,Q)=8$ is even --- every general-position condition of
    \cite[\S3]{AckermanKeszeghRote2022} holds. $\CC(p_0)\neq\CC(p_1)$ and
    $(p_0,q_0),(p_1,q_4)\in\ED$, so $q_0,q_4$ \emph{is} associated to $p_0,p_1$
    under the printed definition, and both cross non-edges hold as well; yet the
    cone dichotomy fails in both directions and the axis through the two apexes
    leaves $a,b,A,B$ in one closed half-plane. \emph{Effect on this proof:} none.
    A pair failing the dichotomy is neither hooking nor hooked, so it contributes
    nothing to $S(e)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:narrow}). \emph{Effect on the
    manifest:} its earlier gloss of the narrow notion as ``edges $+$ cross
    non-edges'' was wrong, since those non-edges are automatic and the dichotomy
    is what carries the content; corrected in \S\ref{sec:a7ext}.
  \item \textbf{The \textsf{A7} encoder's coverage argument was wrong.} The
    oriented-matroid case exhaustion reduced the four $Q$-adjacency cases to one
    representative by a symmetry orbit. That is valid for a $Q$-adjacency in
    isolation and \emph{invalid} in combination with a $P$-adjacency: the
    relabelling generators act in tandem, so the combined types split into
    \emph{four} orbits and only one was solved. Worse, that one was vacuous ---
    its base pattern is itself \textsc{unsat}, so every \textsf{PQ-adj} verdict,
    including the two that matter, held for no reason. \emph{Effect on the
    theorem:} none established either way at the time; the conclusion survived,
    the evidence for it did not. \emph{Repair:} all $25$ combined types
    enumerated with no symmetry reduction and solved separately, $125$
    certificates, hash-bound to their formulas. \emph{Aggravating circumstance,
    recorded because it is the point of this section:} an earlier hostile review
    had checked the orbit structure for a $Q$-adjacency alone and endorsed the
    argument as sound. \emph{Superseded by:} \S\ref{sec:a7ext}.
  \item \textbf{Intermediate pipeline counts are labelling-dependent
    artifacts.} The figures $2{,}300$ and $121$ depend on which representative
    labelling of each exceptional class the pipeline happens to carry; a
    superseded pipeline reported $3{,}522$ and $64$ for the same two stages.
    \emph{Effect on the theorem:} none --- the terminal emptiness is
    labelling-invariant and was re-derived per ordered class pair
    ($196$ pairs, $3$ labellings, $0$ survivors in every case). \emph{Standing
    consequence:} every intermediate count quoted in this manifest should be
    read as an artifact of a particular run, and only the final $0$ as a claim.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Final trust statement}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{
The project currently contains a heavily audited \emph{candidate}
computer-assisted proof of $f(7,7)=38$. The novel finite reductions and the
terminal elimination have been independently implemented, certificate checked,
and in key places Lean-kernel verified. The principal remaining mathematical
issue is \textsf{A7-EXT}: whether the published Observation 6 / Lemma 7 language
licenses precisely the disjoint-status consequence the argument uses. That
consequence has since been independently corroborated --- by proof-checked
unsatisfiability of an oriented-matroid encoding with a verified positive
control, and by exact-arithmetic search --- but its four bridges to the
geometric statement remain the project's own reasoning, so it is recorded as
\stat{very strongly corroborated}, not verified. The same investigation found
\textsc{akr}'s Observation 6 to be false as printed, a defect that does not reach
this argument (\S\ref{sec:defects}, item 6). Conditional on \textsf{A7-EXT}, the
deduction that the auxiliary graph $H$ is bipartite (\textsf{A7-BRIDGE}) is
formally verified in Lean.

Two things should be said plainly alongside that. First, $f(7,7)=38$ is
\textbf{not} a Lean theorem and will not become one by further computation: the
strongest formalised statement still carries \texttt{hclass} and
\texttt{hne40}, and \texttt{hne40} is out of the kernel's reach by a factor of
roughly $75$ even after the fastest re-encoding measured (\S\ref{sec:hne40}).
Second, the mechanisms this project has used --- independent reimplementation,
certificates with standalone checkers, Lean-kernel verification --- are all
mechanisms against \emph{implementation} error, and the record in
\S\ref{sec:defects} shows they work. None of them is a check on whether the
argument is the right argument. Independent expert review is therefore the
binding constraint, and it remains required before the result should be treated
as established.
}}
